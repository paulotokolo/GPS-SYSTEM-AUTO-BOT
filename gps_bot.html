Trade Direction The master switches. These sit above every engine: with a side switched off, none of its engines runs at all, whatever that engine’s own toggle says.
Buys run engines 1–3 off session and swing lows. Sells run engines 4–6 off the highs, as the exact mirror. With both on the bot takes whichever side sets up, and the Market Trend Filter is what stops it fighting the higher timeframe. Sells default off so nothing changes until you deliberately switch them on and test them.
Account Size
Sets risk %, lot size, daily limits and the trailing amounts all at once, to sane starting points for that account size. Everything stays editable afterwards — these are a starting point, not universal numbers.
Stop-Loss / Take-Profit ⚠️ Turning the stop-loss off means a trade has no automatic downside exit except your Daily Circuit Breaker or a manual close — real, uncapped risk on that trade. Leave it ON unless you specifically know why you want it off.
The stop and target are pip distances set under Advanced → TP / SL. Take-profit off just means no fixed target; the trade then exits via Auto Trailing (if on) or the stop.
On open sends the stop and target with the order as pip distances — the format the manual test proves this platform accepts. After open sends a plain order first and sets the levels immediately afterwards, so a rejected stop can never cost you the entry. Either works now that the levels are pips; On open is the tighter of the two because the trade is never unprotected, even briefly.
Auto Trailing ON: once a trade is far enough in profit, the stop-loss automatically moves up to lock in gains as price keeps going your way. OFF: the trade keeps its original GPS stop-loss and take-profit only, untouched.
Once a trade is up "Trail after profit" dollars, the stop starts following price, staying "Profit lock distance" dollars behind it. Tune both to your account size and instrument.
A raw breakeven stop at your exact entry still costs you the spread if it gets hit — a small guaranteed loss, not zero. This adds a cushion on top, so being stopped at breakeven means a tiny win or a true $0. Set it to roughly your spread + commission. Note: trailing only ever tightens the stop, never loosens it, so it can never move your GPS stop further from price than the pip stop put it.

Engine 1 — Priority FVG
BUY COLOUR: CYAN
The core GPS setup: a killzone session closes, price sweeps that session's low, then the first 3-candle FVG after the sweep becomes the demand zone.
Killzone Session: the reference only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the lowest low of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 2 — Add-On FVG
BUY COLOUR: YELLOW
Same FVG zone rule, but the sweep reference is a rolling lowest-low rather than a session box — so it does not have to wait for a killzone to close and re-arms itself after each fire.
Killzone Session: the reference only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the lowest low of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 3 — Demand Zone
BUY COLOUR: RED
Session sweep, then a Red-Green-Green-Green-Red five-candle base. The zone is the first red candle's own high-to-low range — tight and single-candle. Always labelled Demand Zone, never FVG.
Killzone Session: the reference only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the lowest low of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 3 is the demand-zone engine — its method is fixed at the 5-candle pattern and its labels always read Demand Zone.
Engine 4 — Priority FVG SELL
SELL COLOUR: CYAN
The sell mirror of Engine 1: price sweeps a reference HIGH, then the first bearish 3-candle FVG after the sweep becomes the supply zone. Fires on a close back BELOW the zone.
Killzone Session: the reference high only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the highest high of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 5 — Add-On FVG SELL
SELL COLOUR: ORANGE
Same bearish FVG rule, taking its reference high from a closed killzone session. Built for add-on sell entries inside a move that is already running.
Killzone Session: the reference high only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the highest high of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 6 — Supply Zone SELL
SELL COLOUR: PURPLE
Sweep of a reference high, then a Green-Red-Red-Red-Green five-candle base. The zone is the first green candle own high-to-low range. Always labelled Supply Zone, never FVG.
Killzone Session: the reference high only updates when a session box closes. Rolling Lookback: the reference is the highest high of the last N bars, tracked continuously and frozen once a sweep fires.
Engine 6 is the supply-zone engine — its method is fixed at the 5-candle pattern and its labels always read Supply Zone.
TP / SL — pip distances Stops and targets are pip distances from the entry price, shared by all six engines. A buy stops below its entry and targets above; a sell is the mirror. This is the same format the manual test trade proves works on an opening order. Set any field to 0 to leave that level off.
One order goes out per signal and it carries TP1. TP2 and TP3 are logged and exported with the signal as the levels to scale out at, and are used by nothing else — the bot never splits a single signal into three orders.
Market Trend Filter The GPS setup performs best with the higher timeframe behind it — buys in an uptrend, sells in a downtrend. The bot reads the trend off a second candle feed and shows it in the Signal State panel.
OFF (default) means the trend is information only — every engine still fires and the panel simply tells you what market you are in. ON blocks any BUY while the higher timeframe is not in a confirmed uptrend, or any SELL while it is not in a confirmed downtrend, and says so in the log. The two gates are independent on purpose — you can filter buys to an uptrend while leaving sells to fire freely, or the other way round. A signal that is filtered still appears in the log and the CSV; what the gate stops is the order.
